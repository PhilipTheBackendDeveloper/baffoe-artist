The Philosophy of Sankofa: Ancient Akan Wisdom on Canvas

June 15, 2026 5min read

In Akan cosmology, the ancient proverb declares: "Se wo were fi na wosankofa a yenkyi" — it is not taboo to go back and retrieve that which is at risk of being forgotten. In my Accra studio, every stroke of textured impasto and every delicate application of 24K gold leaf is an intentional act of cultural memory, anchoring contemporary Ghanaian identity in sacred ancestral wisdom.

Standing before an unprimed, heavy Belgian linen canvas stretched across six feet of timber in my Osu studio, I am conscious that the act of painting in 21st-century Africa is simultaneously an act of historical continuity and radical innovation. By bridging traditional fine art techniques with computational spatial design in Rhino and Blender, my work seeks to expand what African art can encompass — tactile, monumental, and uncompromisingly modern.

Returning to Kumasi: KNUST Roots & Akan Heritage

My formal artistic foundation was forged in Kumasi at the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) Department of Painting and Sculpture. There, under the mentorship of pioneering Ghanaian modernists, I learned that technique is merely the vessel; the soul of the work must draw from living heritage.

The Adinkra symbol of Sankofa — often depicted as a mythic bird flying forward while craning its neck backward to gently retrieve an egg from its back — is not a symbol of nostalgia. It is an active methodology of progress. To move forward into a globalized future with integrity, we must carry our historical knowledge with us.

To paint on Belgian linen with African earth pigments and pure 24K gold is not merely decorative. It is a dialogue across centuries — rooting our contemporary African identity in sacred ancestral ground.

Baffoe Kwabena — Visual Artist, Accra

The Tactile Language: Raw Belgian Linen & Heavy Impasto

Texture in my work is not an afterthought; it is the primary emotional conduit. I work extensively with extra-heavy portrait-grade Belgian linen because its open weave interacts dynamically with heavy impasto paint:

  1. The Visceral Weight of Paint
    • Applying oil and dense acrylics with palette knives to build sculptural, three-dimensional relief.
    • Creating surfaces that catch shifting natural daylight throughout the day in collectors' homes and gallery halls.
  2. Organic Earth Pigments & Raw Texture
    • Infusing ground ochres, volcanic charcoal, and warm earth tones inspired by the Ghanaian landscape.
    • Allowing the natural imperfections and raw grain of the linen to breathe through the composition.
  3. Enduring Archival Longevity
    • Every canvas is prepared with museum-grade sizing and archival varnishes ensuring century-long permanence.
    • Tested to withstand both humid tropical climates and climate-controlled international galleries.

24-Karat Gold Leaf as Divine Illumination

In traditional Akan culture, gold (Sika) is not just wealth — it is the physical embodiment of the sun, life force (Sunsum), and spiritual royalty. In my paintings, 24K gold leaf is applied by hand using historic gilding techniques.

Spiritual Resonance

The gold leaf does not reflect light passively; it radiates. In candlelit or gallery spotlighting, the gilded motifs illuminate the surrounding dark impasto passages, evoking the sacred regalia of Asante court culture.

Contrast & Tension

By juxtaposing the delicacy of paper-thin 24-karat gold with rugged, raw knife-carved charcoal and oil textures, the painting creates a dynamic emotional tension between the earthly and the transcendent.

Bridging Dimensions: Rhino, Blender & Spatial Modernism

Computational Pre-visualization. Before touching a four-story wall or a complex multi-panel commission, I model the entire architectural space in Rhino and Blender. This allows me to calculate shadow angles, viewing sightlines, and sculptural geometries with mathematical precision.

Sculptural Form in Space. My digital 3D practice directly informs my physical work: the curvilinear forms discovered in algorithmic modeling migrate onto the canvas, giving ancient Ghanaian motifs an aerodynamic, futuristic resonance.

Monumental Public Murals. In projects across Accra, this workflow bridges architectural blueprints with intuitive, expressive spray and brush techniques executed high up on scaffolding.

The Canvas in Dialogue: Live Event Performance

Art does not exist in isolation. Live performance painting has become one of the most fulfilling dimensions of my studio practice:

  • Channeling the energy of live music, celebratory laughter, and ambient sound into spontaneous brushstrokes;
  • Demystifying the fine art process by creating a museum-grade masterpiece directly before an audience in real time;
  • Leaving patrons and newlyweds with an irreplaceable, emotionally charged talisman of their celebration.

Looking Ahead: The Future of Contemporary African Art

We are living through an unprecedented renaissance of contemporary African art on the global stage. As our work is collected from Accra and London to New York and Paris, our responsibility as artists remains clear:

  • Authenticity: Never dilute cultural truth for external expectations. Our heritage possesses boundless depth.
  • Material Excellence: Uphold the highest standards of archival craftsmanship, ensuring works endure for generations.
  • Continuous Evolution: Embrace contemporary tools — from 3D computation to new pigment chemistries — while holding sacred the ancestral spirit.

Baffoe Kwabena Fine Artist & 3D Designer • Accra, Ghana
